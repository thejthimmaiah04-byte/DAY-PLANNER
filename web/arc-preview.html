Today — long-press a handle to lift a task; the ghost slot shows where it lands

i — ii — iv

The plan bends to you, and warns before it's too late

Drag-to-reorder. Every plan row now has a grip on the right. Long-press it and drag — the task lifts, a dashed slot shows where it'll drop, and the planner treats your placement as a pinned override instead of re-sorting it away.

Overcommit warning. When urgent tasks are still open and your work window is within an hour of closing, a terracotta banner says so plainly — reactive triage was the morning-after fix; this is the during-the-day nudge.

A day clock that isn't just decorative. The ring around the current slot turns from gold to terracotta the moment urgent work is still pending, so overcommitment reads at a glance.

  • To reorder: long-press the ≡ grip, drag up or down, release.
  • The gold "14:00 / 15:00" times recompute around your manual order.
  • Red ring or red banner = urgent work is racing the clock.

Weekly Reflection — reached from the Sunday wind-down notification

i

A 30-second Sunday reflection that actually asks "what happened?"

Percentages and streaks never explained why a week went the way it did. Three honest questions do — the best day and its cause, the thing that got in the way, and one concrete adjustment.

Sunday nudge. The 21:00 wind-down notification on Sundays gains a "Reflect on week" action that opens this screen directly. Skip it and nothing nags; it just waits.

  • Kept for 12 weeks so patterns become visible over a season.
  • Any one answer is enough to save — no all-or-nothing form.
  • The numbering here is a real sequence — cause, obstacle, adjustment.

Vision — the daily check logs a parallel streak; the gold banner returns every 30 days

iii — v

The vision stops being wallpaper and starts asking questions

A daily honest check. One tap — Yes, Partially, or No — answers whether today actually moved you toward the vision. It feeds a second streak that runs parallel to task completion, the real difference between a planner and a vision-realization tool.

A 30-day return to intent. After a month, a soft gold banner resurfaces your own words and asks if they still hold. "Still true" resets the clock for another 30 days; editing the statement resets it too.

  • "No" breaks the vision streak — honesty is the point, not gaming it.
  • The 30-day nudge also rides the evening notification when it's due.
  • Your answer for today stays selected if you reopen the screen.

Progress — the vision streak sits beside the task streak; alignment reads as a 14-day strip

iii

Two streaks, side by side — because doing things ≠ doing the right things

The vision streak now stands next to the task streak. You can have a spotless task week and a broken vision streak — and that gap is the insight the old screen could never show.

A 14-day alignment strip colors each day gold, sage, or terracotta for Yes / Partially / No. A run of terracotta on weekdays is exactly the "urgent work crowds out the vision by Thursday" pattern made visible.

  • Gold = served the vision, sage = partially, terracotta = no.
  • Faint slots are days you didn't answer — no guilt, just gaps.
  • Everything here is computed on-device from your daily checks.
